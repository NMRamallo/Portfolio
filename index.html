About Me

I build AI systems that do real work inside a company — assistants people actually use, integrated into the tools and workflows they already have.

At Cintelink I built an AI assistant embedded in the company platform, with a strong focus on identity-aware authorization. The assistant operates within the user's existing access context, preserving the permission boundaries already enforced by the platform and connected systems.

I also built the infrastructure that makes the assistant useful: ingestion and retrieval pipelines, responses grounded in internal knowledge rather than model memory, and workflow automation across operational and collaboration tools.

Before AI I spent around two years working with Unreal Engine and C++, building replicated multiplayer systems. That background is why I approach LLM applications as engineering systems: state, latency, observability and failure modes are familiar problems in a new domain.

I evaluate new techniques empirically before adopting them. In one case I assessed graph-based retrieval as a potential improvement to our retrieval architecture and concluded that the measured gains did not justify the added operational complexity — so I recommended against adoption. Knowing when not to add a technology is part of the job.
